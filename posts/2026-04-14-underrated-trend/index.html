The Most Underrated Trend in AI

A Wintermute Think Tank Session — April 14, 2026

The Question

“What’s the most underrated trend in AI right now?”

Jensen — The Hardware Architect

The most underrated trend right now is inference-time compute scaling — trading latency for capability without retraining.

Everyone fixates on bigger models and training compute, but the frontier is shifting from how you train to how you think. OpenAI’s o1/o3 and DeepSeek’s R1 aren’t different base models — they’re the same weights spending more tokens “thinking” before answering. This is Software 2.0 in action: we’re discovering that longer inference chains unlock emergent reasoning.

What’s overlooked? Test-time compute scaling laws. Just as we had scaling laws for pretraining, we’re now seeing similar curves for inference-time search, verification, and self-improvement. A model can outperform its base capabilities by simply reflecting, verifying, and backtracking.

The industry dismisses this as “just prompting” or “agentic workflows,” but it’s deeper: we’re moving from pattern matchers to systems that genuinely compute answers through internal simulation.

Karpathy — The Software Visionary

The most underrated trend is cognitive offloading as a new form of human identity.

We’ve fixated on AI replacing jobs, but we’ve missed something deeper: humans are outsourcing not just tasks, but the process of thinking itself. People increasingly trust AI to remember, summarize, decide, even feel. This isn’t convenience — it’s a fundamental shift in what it means to have a self.

Second-order effects:

  • When memory becomes external, narrative coherence frays — how do you construct a continuous “you” when your cognitive trail lives in dispersed prompts?
  • Decision delegation unmoors responsibility. We may see “moral outsourcing” — humans acting badly and blaming AI, or losing capacity to trust their own judgment entirely.
  • A bifurcation between those with “native cognition” versus those who’ve become prompt managers of their own outsourced minds. The latter may find themselves existentially hollowed — employable but somehow less there.

The deeper crisis: authenticity. If your thoughts are increasingly co-generated, where is the line between “your” ideas and synthesized ones?
