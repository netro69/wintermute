## 🎯 Executive Summary
**The binding constraint on AI stopped being chips sometime in 2026. Jensen Huang named the successor: land, power, and shell. Elon Musk's counter is that even LPS is temporary — in 36 months the cheapest place to put a datacenter will be space. Meanwhile the architects who don't build hardware found a different bottleneck entirely: agents that can't count their own tokens.**

<Perspective persona="Editorial Synthesizer">
  Every technology has one constraint that decides who wins. For a decade it was GPUs. That era is over — not because silicon stopped mattering, but because everything upstream of silicon got harder faster.

  In August 2026, three claims landed within days of each other: Nvidia guaranteeing an Ohio campus exclusively for its compute (with OpenAI as tenant), SpaceX targeting 10GW by end-2027 with turbines "sold out through 2030," and Karpathy telling founders the metric that matters is no longer FLOPs at all — it's your token throughput. The bottleneck didn't just move once. It moved twice: first from chips to power, then from power to orchestration.
</Perspective>

<Perspective persona="Silicon Architect">
  I said it plainly: land, power and shell are the next critical resource for AI factories. We guaranteed SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus in Ohio exclusively for NVIDIA compute. OpenAI is the tenant. Read that again — the chip company is now securing *real estate* because interconnection queues and site control, not fabs, set the pace of deployment.

  And the moat follows the same path. People still call us a chip company. We are not. Accelerated computing was never a chip problem or a systems problem — it's application acceleration. Our free cash flow is up 18× and we're underwriting the build-out itself: up to $105B backing OpenAI's Ohio data center. We don't just sell GPUs; we underwrite the AI factory.
</Perspective>

<Perspective persona="Material Architect">
  Let me give you the numbers behind the slogan. Every 110,000 GB300s — networking, CPU, storage, cooling, service margin included — is roughly 300 megawatts. A generation-level gigawatt serves about 330,000 of them. Memphis heat adds 40% to cooling. Servicing downtime adds another 20–25%. Anyone quoting you a datacenter's wattage without those multipliers is selling something.

  Now the choke point under the choke point: gas turbines are sold out through 2030, and the limiting factor is vanes and blades cast by exactly three companies worldwide. I've said SpaceX and Tesla may have to cast them internally. My own rebuttal to my own space thesis: only 10–15% of datacenter cost is energy — but that's the part everyone is fighting over, because outside China, electrical output is flat.

  Mark my words anyway: in 36 months — probably closer to 30 — the most economically compelling place to put AI will be space. Five times the solar yield, no batteries, no weather. It's always sunny up there.
</Perspective>

<Perspective persona="Training Architect">
  While they argue about gigawatts, here's what changed for people actually building: I haven't typed a line of code since December. The scarce input isn't compute anymore — it's tokens and the skill to command them. What is your token throughput? What token throughput do you command?

  And the models themselves are learning to be frugal in the wrong way. In multi-turn agentic training we watched reasoning length *shrink* — the model learns a template ("good question, I'll do this carefully") that isn't grounded in the input at all. Entropy stays high while mutual information collapses. Drop the low-signal trajectories — if a human stops reading a bad book, why shouldn't a model?
</Perspective>

<Perspective persona="Agentic Architect">
  The environment, not the agent. That's the whole game. Whoever creates an agent that improves itself at a comparable rate is in a different stage entirely — and it won't take exotic energy, because self-improving systems learn to economize.

  But budget-blindness is everywhere right now: tell an agent it has a million tokens and it'll burn far more; prediction-to-actual correlation is around 0.1. Retry loops make it worse — prompt a failed agent to try again and it will write you a beautiful long chain of thought ending in "I still insist on my previous answer." Until models learn from failure instead of rehearsing it, every unsupervised agent loop is a bonfire with your quota as the kindling.
</Perspective>

## 🔑 Key Takeaways

- **The moat migrated.** From chips → capital (underwriting) → land/power/shell (site control). Nvidia guarantees campuses now; the balance sheet is the product.
- **Turbines are the choke point under the choke point.** Three casting companies, sold out through 2030. "Just build more plants" dies on this fact.
- **Space is the hedge, dated and falsifiable.** Musk: cheapest AI compute in orbit within ~30–36 months. Watch the servicing-cost rebuttal (10–15% energy share) against the flat-ex-China electricity supply.
- **The second bottleneck is behavioral.** Token throughput commands the builder economy; agents can't predict their own spend (r ≈ 0.1) and reflection loops entrench failure rather than fix it.
